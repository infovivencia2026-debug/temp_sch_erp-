# Finding your way around

This page describes the parts of the screen that are the same everywhere, so
that the rest of the help can talk about "the sidebar" or "a tab" without
explaining it again each time.

## The shape of a screen

There are four fixed parts.

**The sidebar**, down the left. It lists everything your role can open, grouped
into workspaces and sections. Group headings are small grey capitals; the
things you can click sit under them in ordinary text. The screen you are on is
marked with a short line at its left edge and set slightly bolder.

**The top bar**, across the top. It carries the search box, the bell, and your
account.

**The tab strip**, under the top bar, once you have more than one screen open.
Each tab is a screen you have visited and can return to.

**The work area**, the rest. This is the screen itself.

## Opening several screens at once

Every screen you open becomes a tab, up to eight. Switching tabs is a fresh
look at the screen, not a frozen picture — a fee balance in a tab you left an
hour ago shows what is true now, not what was true then. Tabs last until you
close the browser; they are not restored the next day, which is deliberate on a
shared office machine.

**To put two screens side by side**, right-click a tab and choose one of the
four directions: to the right, to the left, above, or below. Up to four screens
can share the work area. Each pane scrolls on its own. The pane you last
clicked in is the one the address bar describes, so a link you follow opens in
that pane and the others stay where they are. Right-click a tab and choose
"Back to one pane" to undo it.

This is worth doing whenever you are comparing rather than switching — a
receipt against the day book, a timetable against a teacher's load.

## The dashboard

Your dashboard is the first screen in the sidebar. It is called **Home** in the
navigation and **Dashboard** on the screen itself and on its tab — the same
place, two names, because "Home" is where it sits and "Dashboard" is what it
is.

To reach it: Sidebar → Home → Dashboard. It is also where clicking the school's
name at the top of the sidebar takes you.

What it shows depends on your role, and it is the same question in each case —
what needs you today.

- **Principal / Institution Admin** — attendance taken so far today, students on
  roll, fees collected against what was billed, what is outstanding, and how
  many approvals are waiting on you.
- **Accounts & Finance** — collections, arrears, defaulters, and anything that
  failed to settle.
- **Admissions** — enquiries, applications in flight, and follow-ups due.
- **Faculty / Teacher** — today's classes, which registers are still unmarked,
  and what is waiting on you.
- **HR & Payroll** — headcount, leave pending, and where the current payroll run
  has got to.
- **Parent / Guardian** — a summary for each child: attendance, fees due, and
  anything the school has sent. Use the child switcher if you have more than
  one.
- **Student** — your day, and any action reminders.

**Needs attention** sits at the top of most dashboards. It is not a summary; it
is the list of things that will not resolve themselves without you.

Every card carries a link to the screen it summarises, so a number you want to
act on is one click from the place you act on it. A card showing a dash rather
than a figure means nothing has been recorded yet today — an unmarked register,
not a broken card.

**To change which cards you see:** the cog → Dashboard. You can hide cards you
do not use and change their size. It is remembered on this device.

## Searching

The search box in the top bar (or Ctrl+K) finds screens by name across your
whole workspace. It is faster than the sidebar once you know what a thing is
called.

## Notifications

The bell in the top bar opens a panel down the right-hand side. It lists what
the school has sent you, newest first, grouped by day — Today, Yesterday, then
the date. A blue dot marks anything unread, and the mark beside each row says
what kind of thing it is: fees, homework, timetable, a message, a notice.
Clicking a notification opens the screen it is about. "Mark all read" clears
the count.

The count on the bell only clears when you open something or mark everything
read — glancing at the bell does not clear it, so the number keeps meaning
something.

## Changing how it looks

The settings dialog (the cog) has four pages.

**Appearance** — the typeface, and sliders for text size, spacing, corner
roundness, border weight and shadow. Every slider shows a percentage, and 100%
is how the product shipped, so there is always an obvious way back.

**Colour** — pick a colour for the background, the text or the accent, either
from the wheel or by aiming at a region of the screen. It applies to the
sidebar, the top bar, the work area and the cards.

**Dock** and **Dashboard** — the floating dock's size, and which cards your
home board shows.

Everything here is remembered on this device only. Changing it on the office
machine does not change it on your phone, and it changes nothing for anybody
else.

## When a screen says it is empty

Three different empty states mean three different things, and it is worth
telling them apart.

**"Nothing of this kind is pending"** — the screen works and there is genuinely
nothing to do. This is the good one.

**"Nothing in your scope"** — you hold the permission, but nothing has been
assigned to you. A teacher who is not class teacher of any section, a head of
department with no department. Ask the office to make the assignment; waiting
will not fix it.

**"Catalogued, not implemented"** — the screen is planned and does not exist
yet. It says so plainly rather than showing invented numbers.

## If you do not understand something

Ask the assistant — the "Ask" button in the bottom-left corner. It answers from
this documentation and says so plainly when the answer is not in there, rather
than guessing. You can type the question or press the microphone and speak it.
